Pick your 7 backend keywords

Amazon gives every book 7 keyword boxes. Let us suggest them, ask the AI, or pick from … researched murder mystery and nonogram keywords yourself. Mark one as your main keyword: your title and subtitle are written around it.

    Keyword Searches / mo Competition Competitors Opportunity Pick

    What do these numbers mean?
    • Searches / mo: estimated Amazon searches a month (Publisher Rocket, Amazon.com, November 2026). "<100" means under 100.
    • Competition: 0 to 100 score, lower is easier to rank for.
    • Competitors: how many books show up for that search.
    • Opportunity: demand compared with competition. Higher is better.
    • picture logic tag: the keyword names your exact format (nonogram, picture puzzle, hanjie). Few books combine a murder mystery with a nonogram, so these searches convert well for you.
    How to choose 7 keywords the way Amazon wants
    • Each box holds one phrase of up to 50 characters. Amazon recommends short phrases in the order a shopper would type them.
    • Amazon matches words in any order and ignores plurals, so "murder mystery puzzle book" and "murder mystery puzzle books" count as the same thing. Pick keywords that add new words.
    • Words already in your title and subtitle are indexed already. A box that only repeats them adds nothing, so we flag it in Step 3.
    • Mix one or two big genre phrases with specific ones (nonogram, cold case, Christmas). New books win the specific searches first.
    • Never use another brand or series name (Murdle, Murdoku, Murdograms, Picross, Pic-a-Pix...), a misspelling, or anything the book doesn't contain. We removed those from the list already.
    • Sources: Amazon KDP Help, Make Your Book More Discoverable with Keywords and Metadata Guidelines for Books.
