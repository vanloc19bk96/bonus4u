Penguins are flightless birds that live mostly in the Southern Hemisphere. The Emperor Penguin is the largest species, standing about 4 feet tall and weighing up to 90 pounds. There are 18 species of penguins in the world. Penguins are excellent swimmers and can dive as deep as 1,800 feet to catch fish. They huddle together in large groups to keep warm in the freezing cold of Antarctica. A baby penguin is called a chick. Penguins eat fish, squid, and krill. Krill are tiny shrimp-like creatures found in cold ocean waters. The Adélie penguin is named after the wife of a French explorer. Penguins have a layer of fat called blubber that keeps them warm. They can swim at speeds of up to 22 miles per hour. Penguins lay eggs, usually 1 or 2 per season. The fastest swimming penguin is the Gentoo penguin. Penguins cannot fly because their bones are too heavy. Their black and white coloring is called countershading and helps them hide from predators in the water.
